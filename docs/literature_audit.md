**Source-specific novelty verdict:** no theorem-level collision was found in this inspected article. Richter–Vörös (1989) is the direct antecedent for
**fixed-solution, one- and two-scalar parametric stability** of the
stationary-cost Wagner–Whitin model and is cited prominently as such. It
does not contain: a protected prefix class; a moving best prefix-retaining
continuation; compulsory-prefix (tie-destabilisation) semantics over every
optimum; a freely varying `T`-dimensional weighted `L∞` demand ball;
coordinate demand floors; the strong prefix-radius decision problem; or
any complexity classification. This negative reading supports the contribution boundary below. It does not establish exhaustive priority, and the release does not claim that no uninspected source contains a related object.

### Minimax-regret, inverse and partial anti-inverse literature (updated 18 August 2026)

- Zhang, M. (2011). Two-stage minimax regret robust uncapacitated lot-sizing problems with demand uncertainty. *Operations Research Letters, 39*(5), 342–345. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.orl.2011.06.013 — publisher record and detailed section snippets inspected; polynomial under interval demand uncertainty. The later Li–Romero Morales full text attributes an $O(T^7)$ dynamic programme to this paper.
- Li, D., & Romero Morales, D. (2013). *Polynomial time algorithms for the minimax regret uncapacitated lot sizing model*. Optimization Online 4149. Full text inspected. The model chooses one first-stage setup vector before uncertainty and minimises maximum regret against a scenario-optimal setup vector. It runs in $O(T^6)$ with demand and production-cost uncertainty, $O(T^5)$ for demand uncertainty only, and $O(T^4)$ for production-cost uncertainty only.
- Egri, P., Kis, T., Kovács, A., & Váncza, J. (2014). An inverse economic lot-sizing approach to eliciting supplier cost parameters. *International Journal of Production Economics, 149*, 80–88. Full repository manuscript inspected. It infers setup, holding and backlog cost ratios from historical demand–optimal-lot-size pairs by an inverse shortest-path linear programme.

## Inspected claims

1. **Demand-vector stability predates this paper — along scalar rays.** The full Richter-Vörös text parametrises the demand vector by one scalar direction (`d̃ = d + b·d̂`) and costs by another, for a fixed Wagner–Whitin solution; stability is an interval or a two-parameter region.
2. **Simultaneous perturbations and maximal stability regions predate this paper.** van Hoesel and Wagelmans explicitly position earlier work in those terms and study sensitivity of one optimal economic lot-sizing solution.
3. **Fixed-solution combinatorial stability radii predate this paper.** Chakravarti and Wagelmans give general stability-radius results for fixed binary solutions under independently bounded objective-coefficient perturbations.
4. **Sequential/horizon stability predates this paper.** Richter and Weber address sequential stability and monotonicity under a different object.
5. **Polynomial interval-demand minimax-regret lot sizing predates this paper.** Zhang (2011) and Li–Romero Morales (2013) select one setup vector before uncertainty and minimise its worst-case regret against the scenario-optimal plan; the demand-only version is solvable in $O(T^5)$. The protected-prefix radius reverses the optimisation question: it searches for the nearest scenario where two adaptive restricted value envelopes cross. The existing polynomial algorithms therefore do not settle the released decision problem, but they are the closest complexity comparators.
6. **A direct inverse lot-sizing model predates this paper.** Egri et al. (2014) infer cost ratios from observed demand–optimal-plan pairs through an inverse shortest-path linear programme. Its perturbed variables, observations and target solution differ from demand-space prefix destabilisation.
7. **Other robust and stochastic lot-sizing comparators exist.** Chand–Sethi address decision horizons; Hanafizadeh et al. robustify uncertain costs; Li–Romero Morales (2017) parameterise an uncertainty range; Forel–Grunow model forecast evolution; Weijers et al. evaluate plan stability; Guillaume et al. treat budgeted lead-time uncertainty. These optimise plans or horizon information under uncertainty rather than compute the inverse demand distance to loss of a compulsory prefix.
8. **Real-data plan-stability evaluation exists.** Forel–Grunow and Weijers et al. set a higher external-validity benchmark than the synthetic and reconstructed case here.
9. **Generic partial inverse and anti-inverse shortest paths are hard.** Gassner (2009) and Ley-Merkert (2025) establish NP-hardness for the arc-weight analogues of the present decision event; the open complexity question is therefore confined to the demand-induced, ordered-slope restriction — and, by the prefix-to-arc decomposition theorem, to its one-protected-arc core.
10. **Ordinary conformal coverage is exchangeability-dependent.** The beyond-exchangeability and adaptive conformal literature provides different procedures and guarantees under drift.

## Contribution boundary

The release does **not** claim priority for demand sensitivity, stability regions, fixed-solution radii or forecast-horizon analysis. Its candidate contribution is narrower:

- loss of compulsory optimality of a selected **prefix face**, where the best prefix-retaining continuation may switch;
- a floor-aware polynomial frozen-comparator lower certificate on a prefix-deviation directed acyclic graph;
- a polynomial lower-upper bracket with exact closure conditions;
- integration with exact inverse formulations, one-step calibrated revisions and a structural rolling dead-zone theorem;
- the enumeration-complexity theorem for the released escalation, the prefix-to-arc decomposition, the strengthened NP-membership proposition, the single-detour optimal-face witness theorem, the semantic bridge, and the compact production-balance exact model.

The full-path endpoint belongs near the fixed-solution literature and is presented as a special case, not a priority claim.

## Missingness and uncertainty

The source-specific question concerning Richter–Vörös (1989) is resolved by direct inspection (see the comparison table and `docs/richter_voros_inspection_receipt.md`). Remaining uninspected full texts include the same-programme EJOR instalments (Richter 1987; Richter & Vörös EJOR 41) and Richter & Weber (1994). Their metadata indicate adjacent fixed-solution or sequential-stability questions, but metadata cannot exclude theorem-level overlap. The contribution boundary is therefore supported within the inspected corpus and remains bounded by a targeted, non-systematic search.
